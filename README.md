# 🌌 Aurora Borealis
 
### A Beautiful Introduction to the Wonders of Light
 
## 📖 About the Name
 
**Aurora Borealis** is a natural light display that appears in the sky, primarily in high-latitude regions near the North Pole.
 
The name comes from Latin:
 
 
- **Aurora** — The Roman goddess of dawn.
 
- **Borealis** — Derived from *Boreas*, the Greek god of the north wind, meaning *northern*.
 

 
Together, **Aurora Borealis** means *Northern Dawn*.
 
The name perfectly describes the spectacular colours that illuminate the night sky, creating the appearance of a magical dawn.
 
## 🔬 What Is the Aurora Borealis?
 
The Aurora Borealis, commonly known as the **Northern Lights**, is a natural phenomenon caused by interactions between charged particles from the Sun and gases in Earth's upper atmosphere.
 
### How Does It Happen?
 
 
1. **Solar Activity:** The Sun releases charged particles into space through the solar wind.
 
2. **Earth's Magnetic Field:** Earth's magnetic field guides some of these particles toward the polar regions.
 
3. **Atmospheric Interaction:** The particles collide with oxygen and nitrogen atoms and molecules in the upper atmosphere.
 
4. **Light Emission:** These collisions release energy in the form of light, producing colourful displays across the sky.

## 🌍 Where Can It Be Seen?
 
The Northern Lights are commonly visible in regions near the Arctic Circle, including:
 
 
- Norway
 
- Iceland
 
- Finland
 
- Sweden
 
- Canada
 
- Alaska, USA
 

 
Visibility depends on solar activity, darkness, weather conditions, and light pollution.
